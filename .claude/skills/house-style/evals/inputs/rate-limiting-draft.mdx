Rate limiting is one of the most important primitives in modern distributed
systems — and getting it right is harder than it looks. In this post I'll walk
through the powerful approach we landed on, and why I'm passionate about
getting this layer right.

## Context

The platform had grown organically and our organisation had never really sat
down to think about abuse. It could be argued that this was fine for a while.
Every service optimised for throughput, and nobody owned the question of what
happens when a single client misbehaves — which, inevitably, one did.

The seam between the edge and the origin was where this all came to a head. It
turned out to be the load-bearing part of the whole design, and it wasn't
instrumented at all.

## What we did

So what's the answer? We built a seamless, robust token bucket implementation
that sits at the edge. The behaviour under load is now predictable, and it
might potentially scale to several times our current traffic.

The implementation is elegant — a Durable Object per client key, holding the
bucket state, refilled lazily on read. There's no cron, no background job, and
no shared counter to contend on.

It's been running for three weeks and we haven't had an incident since.
